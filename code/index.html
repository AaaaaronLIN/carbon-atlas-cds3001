What this data cannot tell you

  • 2026 is partial. It runs to 31 August, so it is compared with the same months of 2019 — never with a full year.
  • These are estimates. Carbon Monitor models daily emissions from activity data; they are not metered values.
  • National averages hide a lot. A country total says nothing about which regions or households emit most.
  • No per-capita view. The dataset has no population column, so we do not divide by population or invent a ranking.
  • Living dataset. Values are revised after release, so a later download can differ.

GELSI check

LetterQuestionHow we answer it
G · Governance Who is accountable for the numbers? One owned pipeline: SQL → API → page. /api/health and the status pill expose outages; every chart names its source table.
E · Ethical Could the display mislead? Partial years are flagged and compared like-for-like; axes start at zero; no cherry-picked date ranges; uncertainty stated in plain language.
L · Legal Are we allowed to use this? Carbon Monitor is fair-use open data with a citation requirement (source paper arXiv:2004.13614); no personal data is stored, so GDPR data-subject duties do not arise.
S · Social Who is included or excluded? Keyboard-accessible controls, high-contrast palette, no colour-only encoding, reduced-motion support, and a light theme that prints and projects well.
I · Implications What changes if people act on it? The brief shows sector and country concentration so effort goes where emissions are — while refusing to rank countries on per-capita terms the data cannot support.

GELSI = Governance, Ethical, Legal, Social, Implications — the framework used by the Yale Digital Ethics Center and the WEF Centre for the Fourth Industrial Revolution.
